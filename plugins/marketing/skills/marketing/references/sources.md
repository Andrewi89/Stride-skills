# Marketing sources

Ideas in my own words, one per source. Read the originals for the detail.

## David Ogilvy: advertising that sells
Positioning first, then one promise, then a simple big idea. Facts and specifics sell, and the headline does most of the work. See `ogilvy-copy`.

## April Dunford: Obviously Awesome (positioning)
Positioning has five parts: the competitive alternatives (what customers do if you didn't exist), your unique attributes, the value those attributes give, the customers who care most about that value, and the market category that makes the value obvious. Start from the alternatives, not from your feature list.

## Geoffrey Moore: Crossing the Chasm
Early adopters buy for different reasons than the mainstream, so early traction can mislead. Win one narrow, well-defined beachhead segment completely, with a whole product that solves their problem end to end. Then move to the segments next to it.

## Byron Sharp: How Brands Grow
Growth comes mostly from reaching more buyers, including light and occasional ones, rather than from deepening loyalty. Two jobs: mental availability (people remember you in buying situations) and physical availability (you're easy to find and buy). Distinctive, consistent brand assets (colours, shapes, sounds, phrases) build memory.

## Les Binet and Peter Field: The Long and the Short of It
Effective marketing balances long-term brand building with short-term activation. A rough starting split for consumer products is about 60% brand and 40% activation. B2B tends to need less brand share. Treat the numbers as a starting point to test.

## Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares: Traction
There are about 19 acquisition channels. Brainstorm all of them, rank by promise, run cheap tests on the top few, then focus on the one that works (their "Bullseye" process). Spend roughly half the effort on product and half on traction.

## Andrew Chen: The Cold Start Problem
Products whose value grows with users need a small, stable "atomic network" to work first. Solve that tiny network, then grow outward. Useful for marketplaces, communities and collaboration tools.

## Wes Bush: Product-Led Growth
The product itself drives acquisition, conversion and expansion. Shorten the time to first value, let people try before they talk to sales, and measure usage. See `product-led-growth`.

## Kyle Poyar and Elena Verna (public writing)
Practical guidance on freemium versus free trial, product-qualified leads and PLG metrics. Search their newsletters for current benchmarks. Treat any benchmark as dated.
